💡 Part 2 · Mini-Lesson (15–20 min)
Why Real Programmers “Freeze” Features
✅ The Big Idea

If you keep adding new features right up until showcase day, the old features often break. Real software teams pick a freeze date — after that, no new features, only fixes.

? Think about it

Would you rather show 3 features that work perfectly, or 6 features where half are broken? Why?

🐛 A Bug Hunter’s Four Steps

1️⃣ Describe it.

What should happen? What actually happens instead?

2️⃣ Hunt it.

Read the code near where things went wrong — line by line.

3️⃣ Test small fixes.

Change one thing at a time and re-run to see what changes.

4️⃣ Document it.

Write down what was wrong and how you fixed it, for next time.

🔨 Part 3 · Guided Build (20 min)
Peer Testing & the Bug Priority Sort

Follow along with your teacher, then work through the steps below together.

👥 Peer Testing Checklist
Step 1 — Swap projects with a partner

You know your project’s secret shortcuts, so you never trigger the hidden bug. A partner will find bugs you can’t.

Step 2 — Try to break it

Click the “wrong” buttons on purpose. Write down every bug you find, even small ones.

Step 3 — Sort the bugs: must-fix or nice-to-have?
MUST-FIX 🚨NICE-TO-HAVE 🎨
Nothing works until this is solved (e.g., crashes, core feature doesn't respond)Looks fine but could be prettier — save for polish day
Step 4 — Fix your top 2 bugs

Use the four-step process from Part 2. Fix the two most important bugs your partner found.

🤖 AI fairness check: if your project has a chatbot or classifier, ask your partner — could someone misunderstand what it says? Does it treat every input fairly? Fixing an AI “fairness bug” counts just as much as fixing a crash!
💃 Movement Break (5 min)
Shake Off the Bugs!
👹 Bug Squash Stretch

Stand up! When your teacher calls out a “bug,” act it out with your body, then squash it and reset.

  • “Infinite loop!” → jog in place until “break!” is called
  • “Crash!” → freeze completely, arms up
  • “Bug found!” → stomp once and shout “squashed!”
  • “Ship it!” → jump and cheer

    Find Your Tier & Get Showcase-Ready

    Find your tier below. Freeze features, hunt bugs, and get your 30-second intro ready. Fast finishers: grab a Challenge Card or help a neighbor.

    Tier A — Foundations · Grades 1–3
    🆎 Goal: 1 fixed bug + a simple spoken intro

    With your buddy, find ONE bug together and fix it. Then practice saying what your project does out loud.

    Your checklist:

  • Buddy tried clicking everything
  • We found one thing that didn’t work
  • We fixed it (or asked for help)
  • I can say what my project does in 1 sentence

    • Sentence starter:

    “My project is called ___ and you play by ___.”

    Buddy tip: one person clicks and points, the other watches carefully for anything weird. Switch roles!
    Tier B — Core · Grades 3–5
    ✅ Goal: Top 2 bugs fixed + 30-second intro drafted

    Freeze new features. Fix your top 2 must-fix bugs from the Peer Testing step, then write your intro.

    Your checklist:

  • No new features added today
  • Must-fix bug #1 resolved
  • Must-fix bug #2 resolved
  • Project starts cleanly every time
  • 30-second intro written below
