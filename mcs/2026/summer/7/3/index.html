Mini-Lesson

What Actually Happens at a Gallery Walk?

A gallery walk isn't a lineup of speeches — it's a room full of little conversations, happening at the same time.

🖥️
You set up
Your project runs and waits at your spot, ready to demo.
🚶
Visitors walk around
Families and classmates move table to table.
🗣️
You explain, one-on-one
A relaxed, quiet conversation — not a stage speech.
Notice something?
Nobody said "stand up front and present to everyone." Why might explaining to one person at a time actually feel easier — and let you explain better — than a big speech?
One-on-one means no audience pressure, you can adjust your explanation to the listener, and mistakes are private, not public — exactly like presenting.

Guided Build · Craft Your Pitch

Could You Explain Your Project in 3 Sentences?

Every great demo answers three questions, in order. Try filling in your own right now — out loud, to yourself.

1
What is it?
"My project is a ___ that lets you ___."
2
What's the AI or coding idea inside it?
"It uses ___ (a loop, a chatbot, a trained classifier) to ___."
3
What are you proudest of, or what would you add next?
"I'm proudest of ___" or "Next I'd add ___."
Try it now: whisper your 3 sentences to your neighbor. Did all three show up naturally, or did one feel harder to find?
Most people find sentence 2 (naming the AI idea) the hardest — that's normal! Practice it once more before showtime.
Get Ready for Questions

What Might a Visitor Ask You?

Families walking by will be curious — some questions come up again and again. Which of these could you answer right now, and which needs practice?

"How does it work?"
Walk through it step by step, like a recipe.
"Where's the AI part?"
Point to the one rule, classifier, or chatbot response that "decides" something.
"What was the hardest bug?"
Tell the story: what broke, how you found it, how you fixed it.
"What would you build next?"
One honest idea — even "I'd add sound effects" counts!
The trickiest one is often "where's the AI part?"
Look at your own project: is it a chatbot answering keywords? A Teachable Machine classifier? An "AI character" that reacts differently based on rules? Find the one sentence that names it.

🤖 Bigger Picture

Why Do Even Professional AI Teams Do Showcases?

This isn't just a school thing. Real engineers and AI researchers demo their work constantly. Why might that be true?

Explaining reveals gaps
If you can't explain a part clearly, that's often the part you understand least — a great signal for what to learn next.
Feedback makes things better
A visitor's question ("what if it did X?") is often the seed of your very next project.
Same idea as an AI model: it gets better by being tested against new examples it hasn't seen. Today, you are the model, and your visitors are the new examples.
So each new visitor is basically a new "test example" — and every explanation you give makes the next one a little better.
