💡 Part 2 · Mini-Lesson (15–20 min)
What Actually Happens at a Gallery Walk?
🎭 Three Simple Moves

A gallery walk isn’t a lineup of speeches — it’s a room full of little conversations, happening at the same time. Check each off as your teacher explains it:

🖥️
You set up
Your project runs and waits at your spot, ready to demo.
Got it!
🚶
Visitors walk around
Families and classmates move table to table.
Got it!
🗣️
You explain, one-on-one
A relaxed, quiet conversation — not a stage speech.
Got it!
💬 Craft Your 3-Sentence Pitch

Every great demo answers three questions, in order. Fill in your own right now — then whisper it out loud to yourself:

1. What is it? ("My project is a ___ that lets you ___.")

2. What’s the AI or coding idea inside it? ("It uses ___ to ___.")

3. What are you proudest of, or what would you add next?

Most people find sentence 2 (naming the AI idea) hardest — that’s normal! Practice it once more before showtime.
🔨 Part 3 · Guided Build (20 min)
Get Ready for Visitor Questions
❓ Practice the Big Four

Families walking by will be curious — some questions come up again and again. Answer each one for YOUR project:

“How does it work?”

“Where’s the AI part?”

“What was the hardest bug?”

“What would you build next?”

Look at your own project: is it a chatbot answering keywords? A Teachable Machine classifier? An “AI character” that reacts differently based on rules? Find the one sentence that names it.
✅ Setup Checklist — Is Your Station Ready?
Project is open and running, ready to demo without extra clicks.
Title screen or label is visible so visitors know what they're looking at.
Instructions are ready in case a visitor wants to try it themselves.
I have practiced my 3-sentence pitch at least once out loud.
I know my answer to “where’s the AI?”
💃 Part 4 · Movement Break (5 min)
Shake It Out Before Showtime
🏄 Stand, Stretch, Reset

Stand up, shake out your hands, take 3 deep breaths. Then answer quickly:

How are you feeling about presenting right now — excited, nervous, or both? One word:

Nervous AND excited at the same time is totally normal — that just means this matters to you!
🛠 Part 5 · Open Build (30–40 min)
Find Your Tier and Polish!

Find your tier below. Start there — then climb! Fast finishers: grab a Challenge Card or help a neighbor rehearse.

Tier A — Foundations · Grades 1–3
🆎 Goal: A working, ready station + a simple sentence

Make sure your project starts cleanly, and practice ONE clear sentence with a buddy.

Your checklist:

  • Project opens and runs without help
  • I can say “My project is about ___”
  • I practiced with a buddy at least once
  • I know one thing I'm proud of

    • Sentence starters to borrow:

    “My project is about ___.” “You play by doing ___.” “I'm proud of ___.”

    Write your one sentence here:

    Buddy tip: Practice saying it to one partner twice before showtime — it gets easier every time!
    Tier B — Core · Grades 3–5
    ✅ Goal: Full 3-sentence pitch + one polish pass

    Deliver all three pitch sentences smoothly, and make one small visual or instruction improvement.

    Your checklist:

  • All 3 pitch sentences ready (what / AI idea / proudest)
  • Title or instructions added to the project
  • Practiced full pitch with a partner, out loud
  • Answered at least 2 of the “visitor questions”
  • Project restarts cleanly if something breaks

    • Pitch pattern to follow:

    Sentence 1 What it is
    Sentence 2 The AI/coding idea inside
    Sentence 3 Proudest moment / what's next

    One thing I polished today:

    My partner's feedback:

    Tier C — Accelerated · Grades 6–9
    🟣 Goal: Polished pitch + anticipate hard questions

    Add a stretch answer for a tough visitor question, and organize your files/README so anyone could pick up your project.

    Your checklist:

  • All Tier B goals complete
  • Prepared a specific bug story (what broke, how I found it, how I fixed it)
  • Files/project organized cleanly (or README written)
  • Practiced explaining the AI idea a non-technical way AND a technical way
  • Have a real answer for “what would you build next?”

    • Think through:

    If a visitor asks a follow-up question you didn't expect, what's your plan?

    What's one thing a professional AI team does before a demo that you're also doing today?

    Stretch: Practice your pitch to TWO different people and ask each one what confused them. Adjust one sentence based on what you hear.
    ⭐ Challenge Card — All Tiers
    🌟 Why Do Even Pro AI Teams Do Showcases?

    Think of a reason a real company would want to show visitors their AI project before it's "finished."

    Try presenting your pitch to a grown-up (not your teacher) and see if they understand the AI part.
