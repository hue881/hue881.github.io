Mini-Lesson — Part 2

The one rule every track must follow

AI Moment

Every capstone must include or explain ONE AI idea.

That could be a trained classifier, a keyword chatbot, or even an "AI character" in your game whose replies change based on rules — as long as you can point to it and explain why it counts as AI.

Game example

An enemy that "decides" what to do based on your score — a simple decision tree.

Counts as AI: it reads data (your score) and picks a rule-based response.

AI app example

A model trained to tell two hand signs apart, or a chatbot that answers 5+ questions.

Counts as AI: it's literally trained on data or matches keyword patterns.

Story example

A character that reacts differently depending on choices you type in — rules that "read" you.

Counts as AI: it interprets your input and branches its behavior.

Think about it: which of your ideas from Session 15–17 could become this AI piece?

Open Build · 30–40 min

Finish your plan — then sketch it

TIER A · Foundations

Grades 1–3

Pick ONE track with a buddy's help. Fill the plan with pictures and short phrases — full sentences optional.

TIER B · Core

Grades 3–5

Complete all four plan sections in full sentences, and sketch what your title screen will look like.

TIER C · Accelerated

Grades 6–9

Break your project into a mini to-do list of 5+ small coding tasks in the order you'll build them.

Challenge Card · Any Tier — Risk Check

Write down the ONE part of your project you think will be hardest. What's your backup plan if you get stuck on it?

Fast finisher? Walk a neighbor through your plan out loud — explaining it often reveals a gap you missed.

Share-Out · 10 min

Show your neighbor — or the class

Default — Pair Share

Trade plans with a neighbor. Read theirs, then ask one question that would make their project even clearer.

Optional — Volunteer

1–2 volunteers pitch their idea to the whole class in 30 seconds: track, one AI idea, and what "done" looks like.

No one is ever forced to present. Sharing your plan out loud — even to just one person — is what makes it real.
