🔄 The Training Loop, 3 Steps

Fill in the missing word for each step as your teacher explains it:

📸
Step 1

🔍
Step 2

🎯
Step 3

Predict → Measure error (“loss”) → Adjust weights — and repeat, thousands of times.
🤖 What If the Training Data Is Unfair?

Remember the warm-up — imagine if cats were only ever white and dogs were only ever brown in your training data.

1. What would the AI wrongly learn?

2. This is called bias. In your own words, what does bias mean for an AI?

🌍 Real story: facial-recognition systems trained mostly on lighter-skinned faces made over 30x more errors on darker-skinned faces. If used for security or hiring, whose responsibility is it to fix — the coder, the company, or the data collectors?

Step 2 — Train Class A (Limited on Purpose)

Train Class A with only your own hand — same pose, same lighting, same background.

Step 3 — Train Class B the Same Limited Way

Repeat with a second hand pose — still narrow, on purpose.

Step 4 — Test With a DIFFERENT Hand or Background

Ask a classmate to test it with their hand, or try a new background yourself.

Step 5 — Discuss the “Generalization Gap”

The gap between accuracy on training data vs. new data is called the generalization gap.

Debate questionYour answer
Was the model “broken,” or did it do exactly what its data taught it?
If a company only has data from 10 volunteers, is it ethical to ship the product anyway?

    ⭐ Challenge Card — All Tiers
    🔍 Bigger Bias Problem?

    Which classifier below has the BIGGER bias problem? Circle one, then explain.

  • A. Trained on 500 photos of only left hands, tested on left hands.
  • B. Trained on 500 photos of only left hands, tested on right hands, and used to unlock a real classroom door.
  • C. Trained on 50 photos of both hands, slightly lower accuracy but tested fairly.

    • Bonus twist: Option C had LESS data and LOWER accuracy than A — but might be more responsible. Why doesn't “highest accuracy” always mean “best” or “safest”?
