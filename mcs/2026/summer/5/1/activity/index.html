🧠 Part 1 · Warm-Up (10 min)
Cat or Dog? You Be the “Model.”
🐕🐶 Two-Pile Sorting Game — No Screen Needed!

Your teacher shows one describing word at a time (“has whiskers,” “barks,” “purrs”). Sort each word into Pile A or Pile B before seeing the next clue.

1. After only 1 clue, how confident were you in your sort?

2. After 5 clues, how confident were you?

3. What changed — the rule, or the amount of evidence?

💡 Big Idea: You just did what a machine learning model does — you started unsure, and got more confident as more examples came in. Today the computer does this live, on camera, in front of you.
💡 Part 2 · Mini-Lesson (15–20 min)
Three Words That Explain Almost All of AI
📚 Training Data · Model · Prediction
1. Training Data
The examples you show the computer — pictures, sounds, or poses, each labeled with a name.
2. Model
The pattern the computer builds by comparing all those examples — its “trained brain” for this one task.
3. Prediction
The model’s best guess on something new, plus how confident it is.

Question to hold onto: if a model was only shown 5 examples, would you trust its prediction as much as one shown 500? Why?

🖥️ Meet Google Teachable Machine

Runs right in the browser at teachablemachine.withgoogle.com — no install, no code. It has three project types: Image, Sound, or Pose. Today we train an Image model with two classes, and everything stays on your own computer.

Nothing you record gets sent anywhere. It’s a real neural network, just made visible and touchable.

    🤖 AI Moment — Why Does Confidence Change?

    With only 2 examples per class, a model might guess Thumbs Up 54% / Thumbs Down 46% — barely sure. With 40 examples per class, that same model can reach 98% / 2% — very sure.

    2 examples/class
    Thumbs Up54%
    40 examples/class
    Thumbs Up98%

    If your model is 98% confident but only ever saw YOUR hand, what happens when a classmate with different skin tone or lighting tries it?

    🔨 Part 3 · Guided Build (20 min)
    Build: Thumbs-Up vs. Thumbs-Down

    Follow along with your teacher. Check off each step as you complete it.

    🛠 5-Step Build Checklist
    Step 1 — Open Teachable Machine

    Go to teachablemachine.withgoogle.com → Get Started → Image Project → Standard.

    Step 2 — Name Two Classes

    Class 1: “Thumbs Up.” Class 2: “Thumbs Down.”

    Step 3 — Record Webcam Samples per Class

    Click the record button on each class and watch the model learn live.

    Step 4 — Click “Train Model”

    Stay on the tab! Training happens right on your own computer.

    Nothing leaves your computer — the training happens locally in your browser.
    Step 5 — Test It Live — Watch the Confidence Bars

    Show a thumbs-up, then thumbs-down. Watch the percentages flip.

    🏁 Try it: click the record button 15 times for one class and 0 times for the other — what happens to the bars? Write down what you notice below.
    💃 Movement Break (5 min)
    Stand Up — Be the Model!
    👥 Be the Model Activity

    One student is the “Model.” The class shows one clue-word at a time. The Model guesses out loud after each clue.

  • After clue 1, the Model guesses out loud — usually low confidence
  • After clue 3, guess again — everyone claps if confidence rises
  • After clue 5, final guess — the class reveals the answer

    • Notice: more clues (more data) almost always beats one lucky guess — same rule as the computer. What did that feel like being the Model?

    🎤 Part 6 · Share-Out (10 min)
    Show Your Neighbor Your Trained Model!
    👥 Pair-Share Protocol

    Turn to your neighbor. Show your live prediction and let them try to “trick” your model into being wrong. Then ask each other:

    3 questions to ask your partner:

    1. What two classes did you train?

    2. How many samples per class — and did more samples help?

    3. Could you make it fail on purpose? How?
