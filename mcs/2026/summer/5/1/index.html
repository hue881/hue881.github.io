Warm-Up · No Screens · 10 min

Cat or dog? You be the "model."

Try It — Two Piles

The teacher shows one describing word at a time ("has whiskers," "barks," "purrs"). Sort each word into Pile A or Pile B before seeing the next clue.

Notice This

After only 1 clue, how confident are you in your sort? After 5 clues? What changed — the rule, or the amount of evidence?

Big Idea: You just did what a machine learning model does — you started unsure, and got more confident as more examples came in. Today the computer does this live, on camera, in front of you.

Mini-Lesson — Part 1

Three words that explain almost all of AI

1. Training Data

The examples you show the computer — pictures, sounds, or poses, each labeled with a name.

2. Model

The pattern the computer builds by comparing all those examples — its "trained brain" for this one task.

3. Prediction

The model's best guess on something new it has never seen, plus how confident it is.

Question to hold onto: if a model was only shown 5 examples, would you trust its prediction as much as one shown 500? Why might more examples change the answer?

Movement Break · 5 min

Stand Up — Be the Model!

One student is the "Model." The class shows one clue-word at a time.

After clue 1, the Model guesses out loud — usually low confidence
After clue 3, guess again — everyone claps if confidence rises
After clue 5, final guess — the class reveals the answer

Notice: more clues (more data) almost always beats one lucky guess — same rule as the computer.

Open Build · 30–40 min

Train your own model — then break it on purpose

TIER A · Foundations

Grades 1–3

Train the thumbs-up/thumbs-down model with a buddy. Test it on a grown-up's hand too — did it still work?

TIER B · Core

Grades 3–5

Pick your own two classes (glasses vs. no glasses, two objects, two facial expressions). Record 15+ samples each.

TIER C · Accelerated

Grades 6–9

Add a third class and deliberately train it with only 3 samples. Measure the confidence drop, then fix it.

Challenge Card · Break It, Then Fix It

Train a class with only 2–3 examples. Test it and note the low confidence. Then add 20 more varied examples (angles, lighting) and retrain — write down exactly how much the confidence score changed.

Vocabulary · 6 Key Words

Words every AI trainer knows

Class

A category you're teaching the model to recognize, like "Thumbs Up."

Training Data

The labeled examples the model learns from.

Model

The trained pattern the computer builds — its "brain" for this one job.

Prediction

The model's guess on something new, with a confidence score.

Accuracy

How often the model's prediction turns out to be correct.

Bias

When limited or unfair data makes a model work poorly for some people or cases.

Ethics Seed · Why More & Better Data Matters

You saw it fail. Now let's talk about why.

Too few examples

3 samples per class can't cover every angle, hand shape, or lighting — the model guesses wrong on new cases.

Too similar examples

If every sample is your hand, same angle, same light — the model only really knows YOU, not the concept.

Real-world stakes

Facial recognition and voice assistants have made real mistakes when trained mostly on one group of people.

Who's responsible?

Not the computer — the people choosing what data to feed it. That's a decision, not an accident.

Discuss with a neighbor: name one place you've seen a "smart" machine get something wrong. What data might have been missing?
