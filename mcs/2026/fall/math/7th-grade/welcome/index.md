## 3. Expectations, Responsibilities, and Procedures

### What each session looks like

Every 80-minute class follows the same rhythm: 10 minutes of homework and error-log review with a spiral warm-up, 25 minutes of instruction with worked examples, 25 minutes of guided practice with a partner check, a 12-minute timed drill of 10 questions, and an 8-minute debrief with the homework assignment and exit ticket. Predictable structure means no class time is lost to figuring out what happens next.

### Student responsibilities

- Arrive on time and ready to start; the warm-up begins at minute zero.
- Complete weekly homework.
- Log **every** missed question: the question, the correct answer, the error type (careless, conceptual, procedural, or misread), and one sentence on the fix. Error logs are reviewed at the start of each session and collected on quiz days.
- Write out full solution steps rather than mental math; unsupported answers cannot be diagnosed.
- Show quiz corrections in writing, with an explanation for each miss, at the session after a quiz.
- Ask questions during class rather than after — confusion left unspoken becomes a wrong answer in October 2027.

### Parent responsibilities

- Protect a consistent 40-minute homework block during the week; work done in the car on Saturday morning does not build fluency.
- Notify me by email in advance of a planned absence so I can send the lesson materials and homework.
- Review the error log with your child once a month; it is the single best window into how the year is going.

### Procedures and rules

- **Absences:** materials and homework are posted to the course website; missed quizzes are made up by arrangement.
- **Late work:** accepted, but the value of homework is in the feedback loop, so submit before the next session whenever possible.
- **Phones:** silenced and away for the entire session, with no exceptions during timed drills or quizzes.
- **Timed work:** when the timer starts, work is independent and silent. Pacing target is about 85 seconds per question.
- **Respect:** one voice at a time, no dismissing a classmate's question, and no comparing scores aloud.
- **Academic honesty:** homework and quizzes are the student's own work. Copied answers hide exactly the gaps this course exists to close.
