10–35 · BuildMultiplier language

One move: keep the part that remains

Increase by r
new = original(1 + r)
Decrease by r
new = original(1 − r)
Format check
Convert the percent rate to a decimal before building the multiplier: 8% becomes 0.08, so the growth multiplier is 1.08.
Worked example · Level 3

Discount, then tax: follow the timeline

Model ALevel 3
An $80 item is discounted 25%, then taxed 8%. What is the final cost?
1
Translate
25% off means keep 75%: 0.75.
2
Chain
8% tax means multiply the discounted price by 1.08.
3
Compute
80(0.75)(1.08) = 64.80.
Before calculating
Why must the final cost be below $80? Which multiplier makes that prediction visible immediately?
Successive percents

Equal rates do not undo each other

100(0.80)(1.20) = 96
Locate the changing base
The decrease is 20% of 100. The increase is 20% of what number? Why are those dollar changes unequal?
SHSAT trap
Do not combine −20% and +20% as zero. Multiply the successive factors.
Worked example · Level 4

Reverse the chain instead of guessing backward

Model BLevel 4
After a 20% discount and then 5% tax, the final cost is $84. What was the original price?
1
Build one factor
(0.80)(1.05) = 0.84.
2
Write an equation
0.84P = 84.
3
Undo multiplication
P = 84 ÷ 0.84 = 100.
Fast check
A combined multiplier below 1 means the original must exceed the final. The result passes that size check.
Simple interest

Interest grows from one fixed principal

I = Prt
I = interest · P = principal · r = decimal annual rate · t = years
Two conversions
A percent becomes a decimal. Months become a fraction of a year: t = months ÷ 12.
Worked example · Level 3

Months must enter the formula as years

Model CLevel 3
A $750 deposit earns 6% simple interest for 9 months. How much interest is earned?
1
Convert
r = 0.06 and t = 9/12 = 0.75.
2
Substitute
I = 750(0.06)(0.75).
3
Name the quantity
I = 33.75, the interest only.
Precision question
If the prompt asked for the account balance instead, what additional operation would be necessary?
