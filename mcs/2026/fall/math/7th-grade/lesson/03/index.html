Core Concept

Percent Means “Out of 100”

A percent is a scale, not a separate kind of number.
p% of N = (p/100) × N
Reason before calculating
If p < 100, must p% of a positive number be smaller? What changes when p > 100?
Mental Math Engine

Build Almost Any Percent from 10%, 5%, 1%

10%
Move the decimal one place left.
N ÷ 10
5%
Take half of 10%.
(N ÷ 10) ÷ 2
1%
Move the decimal two places left.
N ÷ 100
Design a route
How could you build 17% in two different ways? Which route creates the fewest arithmetic steps?
Worked Model 1

Level 3: Benchmark Blend

MODELLevel 3
Find 37.5% of 320.
Anchor at 10%
10% of 320 is 32. Therefore 30% is 3 × 32 = 96.
Split the remainder
5% is half of 10%, so it is 16. Then 2.5% is half of 5%, so it is 8.
Recombine + check
96 + 16 + 8 = 120. Since 37.5% is less than half, 120 must be less than 160.
Method choice
Would multiplying 0.375 × 320 be shorter for you? What evidence would prove the two methods are equivalent?

Core Concept

Percent Change Compares the Change to the Start

percent change = |new − original| ÷ original × 100
The denominator trap
The new value is where you land. The original value is the launchpad that makes the change meaningful.
Test the denominator
A rise of 20 has very different meaning when the start is 40 versus 400. Which denominator captures that difference?
Worked Model 2

Level 3: Increase from a Starting Value

MODELLevel 3
A museum’s Saturday attendance rose from 1,250 visitors to 1,450. What was the percent increase?
Find the change
1,450 − 1,250 = 200 additional visitors.
Compare to original
200 ÷ 1,250 = 0.16. The denominator is 1,250 because that is the start.
Convert + verify
0.16 × 100 = 16%. Check: 10% + 5% + 1% of 1,250 equals 200.
Reverse the story
If attendance fell from 1,450 to 1,250, would the percent decrease also be 16%? Explain before calculating.
Reasoning Lab

Same Change, Different Percent

40 → 60
The change is 20. Compare 20 to the original 40.
400 → 420
The change is still 20. Compare 20 to the original 400.
Name the invariant
What stays the same in both stories? What changes? Which feature controls the percent?

Worked Model 3

Level 4: Ratio-to-Angles Chain

MODELLevel 4
The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2:3:4. Find the largest angle.
Count ratio parts
2 + 3 + 4 = 9 equal parts must total 180°.
Find one part
180° ÷ 9 = 20° per ratio part.
Scale + answer asked
Angles are 40°, 60°, 80°. The requested largest angle is 80°.
Connection to percents
Both methods find one common unit first: 1% of a whole, or 1 ratio part of 180°. How is that the same structure?
