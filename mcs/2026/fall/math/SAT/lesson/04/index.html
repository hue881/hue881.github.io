Targets and Method

What Success Looks Like

Line decoder

Turn standard form into y = mx + b before naming the slope.

Relationship detector

Parallel lines preserve slope; perpendicular lines rotate it by a negative reciprocal.

Unit translator

Read slope as “output units per input unit,” then scale by the requested interval.

Function precision

Use parentheses for substituted inputs; read an inverse request as “which input produced this output?”

The question to keep asking
What changed, by how much, and per one unit of what? If you can answer all three, slope stops being a formula and becomes a sentence.

Core Concept · Lines

Slope Is a Rate Sentence

m = Δy / Δx

change in output divided by change in input

Read the graph before the formula
From point A to point B, does the line rise or fall as you move right? Is the slope positive or negative? Which visible triangle records the two changes?
Standard Form · First Look

Do Not Read Slope Too Early

THINKLevel 2
6x + 3y = 15
A student says the slope is 6 because 6 is the coefficient of x. What evidence could disprove that claim without finishing all the arithmetic?
Common trap
In Ax + By = C, the displayed x-coefficient is not automatically the slope. First isolate y; the slope is the coefficient attached to x after that rewrite.
A useful prediction
Before isolating y, ask: when x increases, must y rise or fall to keep the equation balanced? Your sign prediction can catch an algebra slip.
Worked Example 1 · Level 3

Expose the Slope Systematically

DEMOLevel 3
What is the slope of the line represented by 4x + 2y = 10?
1

Move the x-term

2y = −4x + 10

We isolate the y-containing term first. The sign change is visible, not mental.

2

Make one y

y = −2x + 5

Divide every term by 2. Partial division creates a false equation.

3

Read, then verify

m = −2

A negative slope matches the balance prediction: as x rises, y must fall.

Compression move
On test day, write only the two equation lines and the sign check. The explanation lives in your head; the structure stays on paper.
Line Relationships

Same Direction or Quarter-Turn

Parallel

m₁ = m₂

Same steepness, same direction, different intercepts.

Perpendicular

m₁m₂ = −1

Flip the fraction and change the sign: the slopes are negative reciprocals.

Why both operations?
If you only flip but do not change the sign, what stays wrong about the direction? If you only change the sign but do not flip, what stays wrong about the angle?

Worked Example 2 · Level 4

Build a Perpendicular Line

DEMOLevel 4
A line is perpendicular to y = −2x + 7 and passes through (4, 1). Write its equation.
1

Rotate the slope

−2 → 1/2

Rewrite −2 as −2/1, flip it, then change the sign.

2

Anchor the line

y − 1 = 1/2(x − 4)

Point-slope form protects the given point from an intercept guess.

3

Simplify and test

y = 1/2x − 1

Substituting (4, 1) works, and the slope product is −1.

Choice autopsy
A distractor with slope −1/2 flips but forgets the sign change. A distractor with slope 2 changes the sign but forgets the reciprocal.
Linear Models

Slope Comes With Units

output = initial value + (rate)(input)

The unit lens

If x is hours and y is dollars, then slope is dollars per hour. The intercept is dollars at hour zero.

Three-part translation
Say the model aloud: “Start at ___; change by ___ output units for each ___ input unit.” If one blank is unclear, the equation is not yet understood.
Worked Example 3 · Level 3

Scale the Rate, Then Reattach

DEMOLevel 3
A tank begins with 12 liters of water. Water enters at 1.8 liters per minute. How much water is in the tank after 5 minutes?
1

Label the pieces

initial = 12
rate = 1.8 L/min

The question asks for a total amount, not merely the change.

2

Scale the rate

1.8 × 5 = 9

Minutes cancel, leaving liters. This is the added amount.

3

Reattach the start

12 + 9 = 21 liters

The total must exceed both the starting amount and the increase.

A fast error check
If you report 9, you answered “how much was added?” If you report 21, you answered “how much is there now?” Let the noun phrase decide.

Function Precision

Substitute the Whole Input

f(x) = 3x − 2

The container rule

Think of each x as an empty container. If the new input is a + 1, put the entire expression inside parentheses everywhere.

f(a + 1) = 3(a + 1) − 2
Common trap
Writing 3a + 1 − 2 replaces only part of the input. Parentheses are not decoration; they preserve the input as one object.
Inverse Function Meaning

Read the Arrow Backward

What f⁻¹(8) asks

It does not ask you to compute f(8). It asks: “Which input makes f(x) equal 8?”

Translate before solving
Rewrite the notation as a sentence, then as the equation f(x) = 8. What unknown are you actually looking for?

Coordinate Geometry Bridge · Midpoint

Average Matching Coordinates

M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)

Midpoint is a balance point

Average x with x and y with y. Crossing the coordinate pairs creates a point unrelated to the segment.

Visual test
The midpoint should lie halfway along the segment. If your coordinate lands outside the rectangle formed by the endpoints, which arithmetic or pairing error likely occurred?
Coordinate Geometry Bridge · Distance

Build the Hidden Right Triangle

d = √[(Δx)² + (Δy)²]

Not a new idea

The distance formula is the Pythagorean theorem wearing coordinate labels. Horizontal change and vertical change are the legs.

Choose the shortest path
Why do we add squared changes instead of adding the raw horizontal and vertical distances? Which route is the formula measuring: the L-shaped walk or the direct segment?

Debrief · 4 minutes

Autopsy the Distractors

Wrong slope sign

Did the original equation predict a rise or fall? Compare that prediction with the rewritten form.

Reciprocal without sign change

For perpendicular lines, verify the slope product equals −1—not 1.

Rate without initial value

Did you answer the change or the total? Re-read the final noun phrase.

Forward instead of inverse

Did you plug the output in as an input, or solve for the input that generated it?

Choose one error
Name the earliest moment your approach went off track: reading, translating, selecting a rule, executing algebra, or entering the response.
