# MCS Saturday SAT Math Algebra Checkpoint: Annotated Answer Key

Companion to the attached Full-Length Math Practice (Units 0–1, Sessions 3–6): 44 core questions in two 22-question modules plus 4 very difficult bonus questions. Each entry identifies the answer, tested topic, and a brief solution. Question numbering restarts in Module 2. Module 1 questions **5, 10, 15, 19, and 22** and Module 2 questions **4, 9, 13, 17, 20, and 22** are student-produced responses (SPR), entered as a single number with no units, variables, dollar signs, percent signs, or ordered pairs; the others are select-one multiple choice. Bonus questions **B1–B4** are SPR, untimed, and scored separately from the core.

## Bonus: Questions B1–B4

| # | Answer | Topic | Brief solution |
|---:|---|---|---|
| B1 | **16** | Sum and product of roots; parameters | Standard form is $3x^2+(k-11)x-(2k+c)=0$, so $r+s=(11-k)/3$. With $S=r+s$ and $rs=S/2$, $(r-s)^2=S^2-4rs$ gives $S^2-2S=9$, so $S=1\pm\sqrt{10}$. Each value gives distinct real roots and $k=11-3S$; the two values of $S$ sum to 2, so the values of $k$ sum to $22-3(2)=16$. |
| B2 | **−1** | Function composition; absolute-value equations | $f(f(x))=a^2x+ab+b$ gives $a=3$ and $4b=8$, so $f(x)=3x+2$ and the equation is $\lvert3x-5\rvert=2x+t$. The branches give $x=5+t$ and $x=1-t/5$, which are distinct and valid when $t\gt-10/3$. Requiring $5+t\le4$ gives $t\le-1$; the other bounds are looser. At $t=-1$ the solutions are 4 and 6/5. |
| B3 | **12** | Radical equations; inequalities | Squaring gives $x^2-(k+2)x+2k=0\Rightarrow(x-2)(x-k)=0$. Both roots are nonnegative, so both are valid, and they are distinct when $k\ne2$. The conditions are $x\gt1$ and $2x+k\le17$: $x=2$ requires $k\le13$, and $x=k$ requires $k\gt1$ and $3k\le17$. So $k$ is 3, 4, or 5; $3+4+5=12$. |
| B4 | **10** | Systems with infinitely many solutions | Identical lines require $(p-1)/9=4/(p+1)=12/q$, so $p^2=37$ and $q=3(p+1)$. The point $(t,1)$ gives $(p-1)t=8$, so $9t=72/(p-1)=72(p+1)/36=2(p+1)$. Then $q+9t=5(p+1)$; over $p=\pm\sqrt{37}$ the sum is $5(2)=10$. |
